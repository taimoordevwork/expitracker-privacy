Privacy Policy

ExpiTracker (com.expitracker.app) · Effective 3 October 2026

Your spending records stay on your phone. ExpiTracker has no account to create, no cloud sync and no analytics. It does not upload, share or sell what it records, and nothing it reads about your money is sent anywhere. The app is free and shows banner ads from Google, which never receive your financial information.

Your financial data stays on your device

Everything the app records about your spending — every amount, merchant, category and date — is written to a private database on your phone and kept there. It is not uploaded to us or to anyone else. We do not operate a server that holds your transactions, and there is no copy of them outside your device.

Specifically, the app has:

  • No account and no sign-in. You are never asked for an email address, a phone number, or a password, so there is nothing to link your records to you.
  • No cloud sync or backup of your transactions. Automatic Android backup is switched off (allowBackup=false), so your expense database is never uploaded to Google Drive either.
  • No analytics or crash reporting. Your use of the app is not measured or reported. The only part of the app that uses the internet is Google's advertising SDK, described below, and it is given none of your records.
  • No selling or sharing. Your financial records are not sold, rented, or disclosed to third parties, for any purpose.

Notification access, and what we do with it

To record transactions automatically, the app can read notifications posted by banking and payment apps you explicitly choose. This uses Android's Notification Listener service, which you must enable by hand in system settings. Until you do, the app reads nothing.

Access is deliberately narrow:

  • Nothing is monitored by default. You enable individual apps one at a time in Settings. Any app you have not enabled is ignored the moment its notification arrives.
  • Messaging apps can never be monitored. WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Google Chat, LinkedIn and similar apps sit on a permanent internal denylist that overrides your own choices. Even if such an app were somehow enabled, the service refuses it.
  • Notification content is processed on your device and then discarded. The alert is read in local code to extract four things — the amount, the currency, whether money went out or came in, and the merchant name. Those are saved. The notification text itself is not: it is never written to storage, and once the app has finished reading it nothing remains of it.

What is stored on your phone

Records are kept in a private database inside the app's own storage, readable only by this app:

StoredWhy
Amount, currencyThe transaction itself
Merchant or vendor nameCategorisation and learning your preferences
Category, income or expenseDashboard totals and breakdowns
Date and timeMonthly grouping
Originating app package nameDuplicate detection

That is the whole list. In particular, the original text of the bank alert is not among it. Bank alerts often carry details such as the last digits of a card or a running balance; the app reads the alert, takes the four fields above, and keeps none of the rest. Earlier versions did store that text, so that a transaction could be re-checked if it had been parsed wrongly. It is no longer kept, and upgrading permanently deletes any that an earlier version had saved.

Exporting your own data

Settings offers a CSV export. On Android 10 and newer the file is written to your device's Downloads folder; on Android 8 and 9 it is written to the app's own external files folder. The export happens only when you ask for it. Once a file is in your Downloads folder, it is an ordinary file under your control — if you then share it, that is your decision and this policy no longer governs it.

Advertising

ExpiTracker is free, supported by banner ads served by Google AdMob. They appear only on the dashboard and on category pages.

  • Your financial records are never given to the advertising SDK. The app passes it no amounts, merchants, categories, notification content or anything else from your records, and no keywords or other targeting.
  • Ads are non-personalised. The app does not use your device's advertising ID, and asks Google for ads that are not based on your interests or history.
  • What Google collects to serve ads. To deliver ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google's SDK automatically collects some information from your device: your IP address (which can indicate approximate location), device and app information, an app-specific identifier, and how you interact with the ads. Google handles it under its own privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy.
  • Consent in the EEA and UK. If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, you are asked for consent before any ad loads, and can change your choice at any time under Settings, Ad privacy choices.

The app does not request access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone, SMS messages, call logs, or the ability to see your full list of installed apps.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app permanently removes its database and every transaction in it. Because your records are never transmitted, there is no copy of them anywhere else and no deletion request to make. Information Google collects to serve ads is described under Advertising. You may also clear the app's storage from Android settings, or delete individual transactions in the app.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and its ads are not intended for them.
